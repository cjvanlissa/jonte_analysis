Dear Caspar,

My name is Jonte, Terri Pigott's colleague. I hope you are doing well. We corresponded briefly about a year ago regarding MetaForest, and I wanted to reach out again with a question that I think will genuinely interest you.

Terri and I have a manuscript under review at Review of Educational Research examining sources of heterogeneity in mathematics fact-fluency interventions for students with mathematics difficulties. MetaForest is central to the analytic approach. We also implemented a multilevel random forest using the MERF framework (Hajjem, Bellavance, & Larocque, 2014) to explicitly model the nested structure of effect sizes within studies. A thoughtful reviewer raised a legitimate question about whether MERF, developed for clustered observational data, can be appropriately adapted to meta-analytic data, in which level-1 units are precision-weighted effect sizes with known sampling variances rather than raw observations.

It is a genuinely interesting methodological question, and we believe you are the right person to weigh in. I am writing to ask two things:

1. Whether you see a defensible path for adapting MERF to meta-analytic data, specifically addressing the known heteroscedastic error structure that MetaForest handles through its weighted resampling scheme, and if so, whether you would be willing to contribute to that argument as a co-author on the revision.

2. If your view is that MetaForest is simply the more appropriate tool here, I would welcome that guidance as well and would be glad to discuss whether your involvement as a co-author could strengthen the methodological framing of the revised manuscript either way.

I am happy to share the manuscript and reviewer comments if that would help. I am genuinely open to either direction; the priority is getting the methodology right.

Thanks for considering this, Caspar.
